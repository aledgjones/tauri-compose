## Epic Summary And Assessment

- Epic 9 had the right architectural target: harden the real backend-owned VST host seam against HALION-class failures without widening product claims prematurely.
- The strongest execution move was Story 9.2's extraction of a real backend-owned host core. That structural work appears valid and should be preserved.
- The weakest execution pattern was allowing investigation-heavy stories and tracker states to imply progress even when the real product path was still failing and the next decision was not forced.
- In practice, the team stopped getting useful movement from running the epic through its stories. The goal was eventually achieved, but not by completing the epic through the intended process. Epic 9 was effectively abandoned as a trustworthy execution vehicle, and "done" was forced afterward because the technical objective had been reached by on-the-fly work outside the planned story flow.
- The approved 2026-04-12 sprint change proposal is the clearest written sign that the original Story 9 framing had drifted. It explicitly rebaselined 9.3 and 9.4 away from probe-led evidence gathering and toward real host-owned contract hardening on the ordinary path.
- The overall assessment is therefore split:
  - direction: broadly correct;
  - story design and process control: poor enough that the original epic flow stopped being the real delivery mechanism;
  - tracker truth: unreliable and partly forced to reflect the achieved outcome after the fact;
  - technical outcome: achieved, but not through the process the epic claimed to be using.

## What Went Well

- Story 9.2 extracted `src-tauri/src/playback/plugin_editor_poc/macos_host/vst3_host_core.rs` and established a real backend-owned seam instead of keeping lifecycle truth trapped in the POC.
- Story 9.3 explicitly moved toward cleanup-in-place by removing or quarantining fake-success probe residue instead of normalizing it into the product path.
- The sprint change proposal correctly identified that private-GUID aliases, opaque stubs, and similar shim experiments were useful diagnostics but not a credible long-term route.
- The repo's durable rules were directionally right. The instructions already pushed toward real host-owned public-contract hardening and against guessed private-interface success paths.
- Prior-art anchoring was not absent. The artifacts consistently referenced Steinberg first and MuseScore, JUCE, Ardour, and Carla second. The problem was not lack of research; it was failure to convert that research into tighter execution gates quickly enough.

## Main Failures And Challenges

- Epic 9 accumulated too many story states that looked complete on paper while the actual user goal remained unresolved. The most concrete evidence is the disagreement between `sprint-status.yaml` and the story artifacts themselves.
- Investigation-first story framing stayed alive too long. Several artifacts describe progress as classification, reruns, comparison, or narrowing without forcing a direct product-path improvement or a hard stop on an unproductive path.
- The BMAD workflow overhead preserved stale assumptions after they had stopped earning their keep. The method kept generating more artifact gravity around intermediate interpretations instead of forcing convergence on "what must change in the real path next?"
- The accepted story shape made it too easy for "we learned something" to masquerade as "this story is done." That is a serious mismatch for host-boundary work where the only meaningful progress test is whether the real path works better, fails more narrowly, or a dead path is decisively retired.
- The work repeatedly drifted back toward bounded probe logic even after the repo direction had already turned toward ordinary-path validation and cleanup-in-place.
- User confidence was damaged because the artifacts were claiming forward movement while the lived experience was repeated realignment with the same underlying intent.
- The same root issues kept resurfacing across sessions. The agent would surface the same blocker, make a few edge adjustments, claim the issue was fixed, and then a later session would bring the exact same issue back. That is not convergence; it is churn disguised as progress.
- Repo history was sometimes counterproductive. Instead of helping the team step back and compare the current implementation against strict prior art, it sometimes narrowed thinking toward whatever path the repo had already started to artifact, even when that path was wrong.
- Too many strict external-contract facts were treated as if they were still open design questions. In UI or UX work that can be reasonable. In VST3 host integration it is not. The contract is strict, documented, and backed by real examples. The information was there; the execution problem was that the team kept not following it directly enough.

## Recurring Patterns And Lessons

- Real progress happened when the work touched the canonical seam and removed residue. Story 9.2 and the 9.3 rebaseline are the best examples.
- Churn increased when the work focused on bounded investigation without an outcome-forcing proof gate on the ordinary path.
- Prior art needs to be executable, not just cited. For this class of work, each story needed an explicit "copy this ownership pattern, rule out these already-tried levers, and prove the next change on the real path" contract.
- Distinguish decisions from facts. Some domains are subjective and negotiable. VST3 host behavior largely is not. The story should force decisions only where real design choices still exist, not reopen strict contract facts as if they were preference questions.
- Repeated rediscovery of the same issue is itself evidence that the previous slice did not resolve the root problem. Future stories should treat that as a failed closure signal, not as normal iteration.
- Tracker truth matters more in a difficult epic than in an easy one. When the tracker says `done` but the story record says `review` or `in-progress`, the artifacts stop helping decision-making.
- A small concrete fix can matter more than a large amount of theory. The Project Lead's late breakthrough note about the case mismatch in the VST instance call path is a strong reminder that the process was not surfacing concrete, end-to-end reality quickly enough.

## Significant Discoveries

- The right host-core seam already existed conceptually. Extracting it was not wasted work; it was the clearest valid outcome in the epic.
- Fake-success probe residue is not a workable route to support. The sprint change proposal captured this clearly.
- The ordinary product path has to be the primary proof harness. Every time Epic 9 centered a separate probe lane, it weakened decision quality.
- Host-boundary stories need harder closure rules than ordinary feature stories. A story should not close on "better understanding" alone unless that understanding retires a path, changes the real seam, or narrows the failure to one concrete next implementation move.
- Process overhead can become an active defect. In Epic 9, additional BMAD story motion sometimes preserved ambiguity instead of reducing it.
- For strict-contract integration work, repo history is secondary. The primary authorities are the external contract and faithful prior-art implementations. Repo history helps only if it remains subordinate to those facts.

## Open Risks And Technical Debt

1. If Epic 9 continues with the same story pattern, the team risks another loop of artifact updates without decisive product-path change.
2. Tracker drift now makes the planning artifacts less trustworthy, which can hide real unfinished work.
3. The repo still needs one authoritative definition of "done" for this class of host-remediation story: real-path improvement, dead-path retirement, or a narrowed blocker with one explicit next seam.
4. If future host work starts from BMAD ceremony instead of the actual current live failure boundary, stale assumptions will continue to survive too long.
5. The technical seam is still active and valuable, but process noise can obscure whether a new change is genuinely helping or merely generating more narrative.

## Closing Note

Epic 9 was not a failure because the technical target was wrong. It was a failure of execution discipline around a hard problem. The team had the right intent, the right seam, and enough prior-art grounding, but the story machinery kept too many stale assumptions alive and allowed investigation to impersonate delivery. In the end the goal was reached by stepping outside that machinery and doing direct work, then forcing the epic closed afterward. The corrective direction is not to abandon rigor. It is to make the rigor harsher and narrower: one truthful seam, one truthful tracker, one real-path proof loop, strict separation of facts from decisions, and no story closure without a concrete outcome.
