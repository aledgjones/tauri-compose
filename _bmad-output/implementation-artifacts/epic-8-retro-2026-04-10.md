## Epic Summary And Assessment

- Completed stories: 6/6
- Epic 8 delivered the intended capability chain: durable playing-technique score entries, visible score workflow, bounded properties integration, backend-owned effective technique derivation, additive expression-map matching, and routed-transport proof through derived context.
- The strongest execution choice was keeping ownership lines intact from start to finish. Score truth stayed backend-owned, preparation stayed the semantic owner, expression maps stayed a translation layer, and transport stayed a consumer rather than becoming a second semantics engine.
- The most important architectural validation is that the existing expression-map design absorbed the new `playing_technique` condition family cleanly. Adding score-level technique meaning did not require refactoring rule ordering, editor ownership, or transport architecture.

## What Went Well

- Story slicing stayed disciplined. Each story extended one existing seam instead of widening scope across UI, persistence, preparation, and transport all at once.
- The score-model, renderer, properties, preparation, expression-map, and transport layers advanced in a coherent order, so later stories built on stable earlier seams rather than reopening them.
- Expression Maps scaled additively. Story 8.5 introduced `playing_technique` matching through the existing stable-id row model, logical-AND rule semantics, and first-match-wins ordering without a refactor.
- Story 8.6 validated the right thing: the current routed transport path already handled technique-driven playback once prepared context and condition matching existed, so the final story was proof-first rather than plumbing-first.
- Documentation kept pace with implementation. Architecture, data-model, development-guide, and component/workflow docs were updated throughout the epic instead of being left for a cleanup pass.
- Validation discipline remained strong in the story record. The epic repeatedly closed work with lint, type, Rust test, and clippy gates rather than treating backend semantic changes as test-optional.

## Main Failures And Challenges

- Epic close discipline needed one extra sync pass. Story 8.6's proof work was complete before the story and epic tracker state were fully closed, which is a process lesson for future epics.
- Same-tick visible technique coexistence remains intentionally rough. Multiple markings draw on the same canonical anchor and are not individually selectable yet, which is acceptable for the MVP proof but not a scalable long-term UX.
- The hidden-but-semantic rule and deterministic same-tick conflict rule were subtle and needed explicit guardrails. Without those clarifications, UI and playback ownership could easily have drifted apart.
- Multiple stories carried documentation impact, which was the right decision, but it confirms that semantics-heavy playback work has a recurring coordination cost across code, tests, and docs.

## Recurring Patterns And Lessons

- Backend-owned additive extension works. Epic 8 succeeded because each story extended an existing owner seam instead of creating a parallel system.
- Score semantics should be derived once in preparation and consumed everywhere else. The more that rule held, the simpler later stories became.
- Expression Maps work best as a translation layer over derived score meaning, not as the owner of raw score-technique state.
- Focused proof stories are valuable. Story 8.6 answered the architectural question with seam-local proof instead of another architectural reset.
- Documentation updates alongside implementation reduced drift and made later stories easier to scope correctly.

## Significant Discoveries

- Derived score meaning can scale without transport-local state. Once effective techniques were prepared on note events, both rule matching and transport proof stayed simple.
- Hidden playing-technique entries can remain semantically active without breaking the visual directive model. That separation between visibility and meaning is viable and testable.
- Deterministic same-tick resolution by durable ordering (`UniqueId` / last-created wins) gave a stable rule without involving React or transport-local heuristics.
- The routed transport path already supported technique-driven playback once prepared context and condition matching existed. The architecture did not need a second playback path.
