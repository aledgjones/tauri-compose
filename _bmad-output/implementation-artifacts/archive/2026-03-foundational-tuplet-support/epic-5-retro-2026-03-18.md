## Epic Summary

Epic 5 closed the foundational tuplet initiative with a hardening pass focused on regression protection, non-tuplet safety, durable documentation, and repo guardrails. All four planned stories were completed without reopening settled architecture, expanding feature scope, or ending in a late cleanup scramble.

The defining success of the epic was scope discipline. This was a validation epic that stayed a validation epic. The work hardened the product where failures would actually matter, preserved the already-stable shared tuplet contract, and left the codebase in a maintainable post-feature state.

## What Went Well

- Scope discipline held from start to finish. The epic stayed focused on hardening and did not turn into a stealth feature expansion.
- The team avoided a manic cleanup push at the end. Validation evidence accumulated steadily through the story sequence instead of being rushed during closure.
- Hardening stayed close to the owning seams. Parser, rendering, playback, persistence, documentation, and guardrails were reinforced where the architecture actually owns behavior.
- The shared backend-owned tuplet model remained stable. Epic 5 preserved and documented that model rather than forcing new interpretation layers.
- Validation stayed credible and repeatable. Story records consistently cite green `npm run lint`, `npm run check-types`, `cargo test --manifest-path src-tauri/Cargo.toml`, and `cargo clippy --manifest-path src-tauri/Cargo.toml --all-targets --all-features` runs.
- Non-tuplet behavior remained explicitly protected instead of being treated as safe by assumption.

## Challenges And Friction

### 1. Process overhead remained the main source of friction

The friction in Epic 5 was mostly artifact weight rather than engineering instability. Some stable guardrail language was repeated across closely related story records, and proof of validation still lives across multiple local records.

### 2. Strong execution can hide the reason it worked

Because the epic closed cleanly, future contributors could underestimate how much of that smoothness came from earlier architectural discipline. The real success condition was not just good cleanup. It was that the shared semantic model was already coherent enough that hardening could remain local and calm.

### 3. Final closure still depends on explicit record hygiene

The epic itself finished cleanly, but the project still depends on closing the loop in tracking and retrospective records so that the archive-quality final state is easy to trust.

## Key Learnings

- A validation epic succeeds when it stays narrow. Calm hardening is a sign of architectural health.
- Seam-local regression proof is more valuable than generalized coverage churn when the goal is long-term maintainability.
- The strongest protection against future tuplet regressions is not memory. It is durable documentation plus repo-level guardrails.
- Preserving non-tuplet behavior explicitly is part of shipping tuplets safely, not an optional follow-up.
- Good final-epic execution looks uneventful because the hard architectural decisions were made earlier and held.
