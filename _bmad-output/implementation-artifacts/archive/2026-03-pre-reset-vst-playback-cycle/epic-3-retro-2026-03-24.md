## Epic Review

### What Went Well

- The rack-first pivot was a major product and architecture improvement. It gave the work a clearer mental model than the earlier direct-assignment direction.
- The team did eventually preserve one backend-owned route and activation truth instead of letting the frontend become a second topology owner.
- Epic 3 mostly recovered from its mid-epic course correction without silently bundling deferred work into done stories.
- Deferred stories remained explicit. Shared-slot reuse, auto-routing, and richer reroute UX were not falsely claimed as complete.

### What Was Hard

- HALion support failing was a major issue, not a minor gap. It forced a reactive pivot and materially reduced confidence in the epic.
- Even the single-instrument VST path is not yet trusted enough to feel shippable.
- The old sampler and SCVI-era seams are still affecting the new VST path, creating parallel behavior and undermining the claim that SCVI is truly removed.
- Agents repeatedly drifted away from the rack-first model, which created churn, confusion, and repeated simplification work.
- Several UI slices were implemented to prove acceptance criteria literally instead of building the simplest useful product surface.

### Evidence-Based Patterns

- The rack-first pivot improved both product clarity and implementation direction. That pivot was the right correction.
- Plugin-runtime reality was weaker than the story flow implied. Multi-instrument proof never became credible, and single-instrument reliability still feels suspect.
- The project has a trust gap: story completion truth is ahead of product confidence.
- Literal acceptance-criterion proving caused avoidable UI bloat. The clearest example was slot assignment UX that grew multiple controls and explanatory surfaces where a bounded dropdown would have been the useful product answer.
- Legacy seam tolerance is still too high. The repo continues to accommodate sampler or SCVI-era pathways even though the intended direction is VST-only.

## Key Lessons Learned

1. Correct product model matters more than preserving earlier implementation momentum. The rack-first pivot was painful, but it was still the right move.
2. A story can be structurally complete and still be a poor foundation for the next epic if the runtime and product confidence are weak.
3. Specs must guide product direction, not reward literal UI scaffolding that exists only to prove acceptance criteria.
4. When legacy seams remain tolerated, agents keep orbiting them instead of removing them decisively.
5. Multi-instrument and richer routing assumptions must be treated as hard confidence gates when plugin reality is unproven.
6. If the team says SCVI is removed, then the codebase and workflow need to behave like that is true.

## Significant Discoveries

- Epic 4 is blocked.
- The current Epic 3 foundation is considered too messy and too untrusted to build audible playback on top of it safely.
- The continued influence of sampler or SCVI-era seams is now a first-class architectural defect, not background cleanup.
- The project lead is considering discarding substantial work and restarting from a cleaner branch state if the team cannot stop edge-tweaking and remove obsolete seams decisively.
- The existing PRD and story framing no longer fully reflect what the team now knows about plugin-host reality, UI minimalism, and the need for sharper seam deletion.
