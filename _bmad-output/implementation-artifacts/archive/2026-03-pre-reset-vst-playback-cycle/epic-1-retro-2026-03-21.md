## Epic Review

### What Went Well

- The epic followed a strong retirement sequence: runtime first, then user workflow, then tooling, then docs, then repo-wide verification.
- Story scope boundaries were unusually clear. Each story document explicitly prevented spillover into neighboring cleanup work, which kept the implementation focused.
- Validation discipline strengthened across the epic. Early stories surfaced unrelated lint or clippy debt; later stories completed with full repo checks passing cleanly.
- The team removed active SCVI ambiguity at the root instead of keeping transitional fallbacks. That improved the product story and the developer story at the same time.
- Documentation and maintainer guidance were ultimately brought back into alignment with the codebase, which reduced future misreads of the active playback direction.

### What Was Hard

- Story sequencing drifted near the end of the epic. Story 1.5 closed before Story 1.4 review was reflected in the tracker, which briefly made the tracker narrative stronger than the literal status truth.
- Several stories exposed hidden residue outside their primary seam, especially stale tests, stale docs, and pre-existing clippy findings.
- The remaining SCVI code could not be handled as a pure delete exercise. The team had to classify references carefully into active defects, bounded legacy internals, and historical records.
- The docs lagged the runtime/tooling cleanup for part of the epic, which created temporary confusion about what was actually current behavior.

### Evidence-Based Patterns

- Root-cause cleanup worked better than surface relabeling.
- Repo-wide search plus explicit classification was an effective verification technique for retirement work.
- Foundation changes benefited from full validation gates, even when a story looked documentation-only or tooling-only on paper.
- Tracker hygiene matters. Verification and closure work should not outrun prerequisite review-state updates.

## Key Lessons Learned

1. Retirement epics work best as layered passes: runtime, UI/workflow, tooling, docs, then verification.
2. Each retirement story should carry validation at the owning seam so cleanup does not silently move defects downstream.
3. Documentation truth and sprint-status truth are part of the product foundation, not optional polish.
4. Legacy references need explicit classification. Blanket deletion is risky; blanket tolerance is worse.
