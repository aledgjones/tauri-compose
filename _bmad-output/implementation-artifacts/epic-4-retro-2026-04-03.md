## Epic Review

### What Went Well

- Epic 4 achieved the product goal that mattered: routed score material became audibly usable from the core score workflow instead of through a detached plugin-only proof path.
- The team corrected architecture instead of defending a weak proof. When Story 4.2 exposed that the burst-render path was not a dependable foundation, the work shifted to Story 4.3 and established the continuous render engine that later playback work can trust.
- Backend ownership stayed intact through the epic. Playback preparation, activation truth, engine behavior, and route-capability honesty remained Rust-owned rather than drifting into frontend-owned approximations.
- The score-first UX stayed bounded. Mix controls and playback surfaces remained compact, route-first, and honest instead of expanding into proof-oriented or DAW-like surfaces.
- Documentation and agent guidance were updated alongside the implementation work, which reduced ambiguity once the corrected playback shape became clear.

### What Was Hard

- Architecture direction still needed prompting during execution. The user correctly called out that the foundation became solid, but best practices should have been applied earlier instead of being reinforced mid-flight.
- Story 4.2 revealed a real gap between an implementation that could pass bounded checks and an implementation that was trustworthy enough to carry forward. That forced an in-epic correction rather than a straight-line delivery path.
- Playback work remained highly sensitive to ownership boundaries. Small mistakes in where transport, audition, editor hosting, and engine processing lived could have reopened the exact ambiguity Epic 1 through Epic 3 had worked to remove.
- Sprint bookkeeping drift repeated again. All Epic 4 stories reached `done`, but `epic-4` still sat at `in-progress` and `epic-4-retrospective` still sat at `optional` before this closeout.

### Continuity Insights

## Key Lessons Learned

1. Playback stories need explicit architecture and best-practice framing before implementation starts. Mid-epic prompting worked, but it was avoidable.
2. Honest correction is a strength. Replacing the weaker burst-render proof with the continuous engine was the right call and materially improved the foundation.
3. Compact score-context UX works when backend capability truth is strong. Epic 4 reinforced that clarity came from better ownership and honesty, not more UI.
4. Audition readiness and transport trust are related but not identical. Keeping those seams distinct prevented false gating and misleading UX.
5. Sprint closeout discipline is still a live process issue. Epic and retrospective statuses must be updated in the same pass as the final story closeout.

## Significant Discoveries

- Story 4.2's original burst-render path was not a trustworthy long-term owner for audible playback. The epic needed a continuous render engine before the audible foundation could honestly be considered complete.
- The product now has a clearer playback baseline: transport is a scheduling and state-transition seam, while the engine owns live render behavior and stream-adjacent processing.
- Epic 4 proved that compact route-first mix controls can remain honest and useful without broadening the UX surface.
- The main carry-forward risk is process discipline, not product viability. Epic 5 does not need a wholesale rewrite, but its stories should start from the corrected engine architecture explicitly.
