## Epic Summary And Assessment

- Completed stories: 5/5
- Epic 7 delivered the intended release-hardening outcome: one canonical support baseline, discovery and host validation grounded in owner seams, targeted host-lifecycle repair, transport-trust evidence, and a final bounded support verdict.
- The strongest execution choice was sequencing. The team defined the baseline first, gathered evidence against it, isolated the host instability into a focused follow-up, and only then published the final verdict.
- The biggest remaining limitation is explicit rather than hidden: Compose can now claim a composition-trustworthy backend-owned routed playback seam, but it still cannot claim concrete real-library support for `BBC Symphony Orchestra.vst3` or `Originals - Intimate Grand Piano.vst3` until maintainer manual proof is captured.

## What Went Well

- Epic 7 kept one canonical truth surface in `docs/playback-support-baseline.md` and forced surrounding docs to point at it instead of carrying competing support matrices.
- Discovery, host, and transport validation all stayed tied to backend owner seams. The epic did not add proof-only UI, a second compatibility taxonomy, or frontend-owned support truth.
- Story slicing improved materially compared with the broader workflow churn in Epic 6. When manual validation exposed host instability after Story 7.2, the team created Story 7.2a rather than burying the work in a vague follow-up note.
- The team handled the native editor-host instability as a systems problem, not a symptom chase. Story 7.2a identified lifecycle ownership faults and applied structural fixes instead of papering over reopen failures.
- The final support verdict stayed honest. Epic 7 explicitly preserved `discovered but unvalidated` posture where bundle-level proof was still missing instead of converting seam-level automation into broader release claims.

## Main Failures And Challenges

- Epic 7 started with ambiguity between planning language about library families and the concrete bundle-level evidence needed for an honest release claim.
- Automated owner-seam tests were not sufficient to expose the real-library reopen instability. Manual maintainer validation surfaced a gap that automation alone had not closed.
- Bundle-level maintainer proof still trails seam-level automation at epic close. That is now explicit carry-forward work rather than hidden uncertainty, but it is still unfinished evidence.
- Epic closeout discipline needed one final correction pass. Story 7.4 correctly left Epic 7 open pending retrospective completion, so the sprint tracker required this closeout update to align with the actual delivery state.

## Recurring Patterns And Lessons

- Support claims must stay backend-truth-driven. Across every story, the healthy pattern was the same: owner seam first, evidence second, documentation third.
- Concrete bundle vocabulary matters more than generic compatibility language. Epic 7 became much clearer once `BBC Symphony Orchestra.vst3`, `Originals - Intimate Grand Piano.vst3`, `Reverb.vst3`, and `ProEQ.vst3` replaced vague family-level wording.
- Seam proof and bundle proof are different gates. Story 7.3 proved the backend-owned transport contract is composition-trustworthy for the bounded scenario matrix, but that did not automatically promote concrete real-library bundles.
- Manual maintainer validation remains necessary for release claims that name specific libraries. Epic 7 confirmed that automated seam coverage is necessary but not always sufficient.
- Structural lifecycle issues should be repaired holistically. The successful 7.2a fixes came from tightening detach, reattach, session uniqueness, and container cleanup together.

## Significant Discoveries

- The most important Epic 7 discovery is that release credibility is a seam verdict before it is a bundle verdict. Compose can now trust the backend-owned routed playback seam for the bounded scenario matrix without pretending that every representative real-library bundle is already `known-good`.
- Manual maintainer validation remains part of the support gate for concrete library claims. Epic 7 proved that a green automated suite does not replace repeated host-plus-transport proof on named bundles.
- The bounded native editor-host instability was caused by lifecycle ownership faults, not by a missing broader host architecture. The fixes were specific to detach activation, session uniqueness, container cleanup, and safe reopen behavior.
- One canonical support baseline document materially improved planning, QA, and release communication quality. It reduced drift and made support language enforceable.
