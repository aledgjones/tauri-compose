## Overall Outcome

Epic 4 is accepted as complete and leaves the project in a clean state for future work. The epic delivered a disciplined validation pass across document persistence, desktop workflow behavior, playback scope, and offline-first architectural boundaries without forcing new persistence semantics, migration work, or platform-scope expansion.

The strongest outcome is that Epic 4 proved the multi-flow score view remained a presentation-layer capability rather than quietly turning into a persistence, playback, or cloud-coupled redesign. It increased confidence in the current direction and made the preserved boundaries explicit.

## What Went Well

- The epic had tight scope and clear outcomes, which made review and validation concrete.
- Each story stayed close to an existing seam instead of expanding product scope: load/save, native desktop workflow, active-flow playback, and offline-first architecture.
- Regression protection is now in a strong place for the validated areas. The epic added focused tests where they were most useful and avoided speculative churn where an audit was the right tool.
- The team preserved the existing product model: `.soloc` persistence stayed authoritative, `ui.flow_id` remained the playback-relevant active-flow slot, and multi-flow rendering stayed derived from existing score and UI state.
- Claims about preserved behavior were backed by evidence. The epic relied on audits, focused tests, and manual verification instead of assumption.

## Challenges And Friction

### 1. The validation epic came later than ideal

The main limitation was sequencing rather than execution quality. Epic 4 functioned as a valuable confidence-building validation pass, but its leverage would have been higher if more of these checks had been established before or alongside the implementation epics they were protecting.

### 2. Guardrails were confirmed after the work rather than before it

By the end of Epic 4, the project had strong coverage and explicit boundary checks for the validated seams. The missed opportunity was not a lack of quality in the final state, but that some of this guardrail thinking arrived after the main implementation risk had already passed.

### 3. Future regression discovery should stay selective

The retro did not identify broad testing gaps that need immediate follow-up. The practical concern is making sure future work starts with a quick review of new failure modes, rather than expanding coverage speculatively between epics.

## Key Learnings

- Validation work still has high value even when it lands after implementation, because it turns confidence into evidence and clarifies preserved system boundaries.
- The better sequencing pattern is to front-load a brief regression-hole and boundary review before future implementation work begins.
- Strong coverage does not mean every future risk is already handled. The right practice is to scan for new exposed edges when new work is defined.
- Focused audits are the right tool when the intended outcome is to prove that an existing architectural boundary held and no code change is required.
- The team is in a good place on regression protection for the current multi-flow scope and should avoid adding low-value checks without a new risk surface to justify them.

## Epic 3 Follow-Through

Epic 3 retrospective lessons were applied well in Epic 4.

- Story work consistently started from the existing architectural seam it needed to preserve.
- The evidence standard from Epic 3 carried forward: preserved behavior was validated through tests, audits, and manual confirmation rather than inferred.
- The team avoided introducing parallel frontend-owned or persistence-owned concepts while reviewing multi-flow impact.

Partially applied:

- The guardrail mindset was applied effectively inside Epic 4, but in hindsight the epic itself would have delivered even more value if this validation-first framing had come earlier in the roadmap.

## Future Work Preparation

No Epic 5 is defined yet, and the retrospective did not identify immediate remediation work that must happen now.

The preparation principle for future work is straightforward:

1. Before a new implementation epic begins, run a short regression-hole and boundary review against the new risk surface.
2. Add only the checks needed for those newly exposed risks.
3. Reevaluate again when the next body of work is defined rather than expanding coverage speculatively now.
